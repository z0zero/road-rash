# Difficulty is the race's one configuration besides its seed

v1.0.0's race is fair to a player who rides a decent line or hits back, but a first-timer rides at the back alone, and a player who has qualified wants more. Rather than make the one race easier for everyone, the game offers a difficulty, Easy, Normal or Hard, chosen on the title screen, and the Race module takes it with the seed: `createRace({ seed, difficulty })`. This is the race configuration ADR 0004 said would come when something needed one. A difficulty is a name, not numbers the game passes in: each is a small, fixed set of named tuning values in the Race module (the opponents' pace, how often they attack the player, their breathers and whether a player fighting them back gets them too, their patience and catch-up), so every race the game can create is one the Race module's tests can create, and the same seed, difficulty and inputs replay the same race. Only the opponents change: the track, the start grid and the rules for the traffic and the cops are the same on every difficulty, though the cars and cops then react to how the opponents ride, so one seed's traffic differs from one difficulty to the next. Normal keeps v1.0.0's tuning and is the default: a race recorded with v1.0.0 replays on it byte for byte unless a later fix to the rules changes it, as those fixes change every difficulty alike, and `?seed=` replays take `&difficulty=`; a seed alone, as in v1.0.0's addresses, replays on Normal, whatever difficulty the player last chose. Each difficulty keeps its own personal best, since times on one don't compare with times on another, and a v1.0.0 save's personal best becomes Normal's. A difficulty must not grow into a career mode's settings, such as other tracks or upgrades: those would revisit this decision.

Easy's opponents ride at 0.89 of their pace on Normal, more than the "a little slower" first planned for a first race: at 0.94, the first guess, a novice riding at 75% of a safe pace still finished 12.9th on average, not mid-field, and at 0.89 they finish 7.7th. Both paces were tried with Easy's own catch-up, which already holds the field nearer than Normal's: it starts 25 m from the player rather than 75 m, and is at its full 8% from 150 m rather than 400 m. Catch-up stronger still, such as a larger share, the other way to hold the field nearer a novice, was not tried, since it brings the fighters nearer too.

Hard fights harder, attacking twice as often with 1 s breathers, so its brawlers and bullies give up on a player who doesn't hit back after four of their hits in a row: a bully's club takes a fresh player's stamina in four swings, and with six it knocked off a player who never hit back every other race at an average pace, with the hit-back prompt on screen. (A fighter coming back, or the next one taking its turn, can still knock off a player whose stamina is low.) On Hard and Easy an opponent also gives its breather to a player who is fighting it back, a tuning choice rather than a fix: hitting back there must not bring the attacks on faster. On Hard it cuts the hits a player who hits back takes, by 0.6 a race at an expert pace over 96 races and 3.1 at an average one, and leaves qualifying as it was; on Easy, first-timers are the players the hit-back prompt tells to hit back. Normal keeps v1.0.0's brawl, in which a player who hits back gets no breather.
